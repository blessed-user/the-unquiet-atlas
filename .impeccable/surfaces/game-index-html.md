# Field Notes Game Surface

Mode: Experience

Audience: desktop players and visual judges.
Task: compare paired original expedition exposures, mark differences, use a limited hint, and complete five linked scenes.
Constraints: static offline files, mouse and keyboard input, clear remaining counter, visible found markers, responsive desktop composition, reduced-motion support.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The playfield is a working conservator's light table holding one physical contact sheet, not a game dashboard wrapped around two images. It refuses the generic centered game card, decorative hero, and floating HUD template; labels, controls, progress, and photographs all belong to the same catalogued artifact.

OWN-WORLD: Deep bottle-green blackout surrounds a warm illuminated table. Bone photo borders, oxidized brass controls, tomato-red grease-pencil marks, faded cyan stamps, and graphite copy form a committed full palette. Narrow grotesk labels, stamped numerals, clipped paper tabs, and fine registration marks replace generic cards and pills.

STORY: The player opens a recovered expedition case, compares left and right field exposures, and restores five incomplete records. Every correct observation leaves a grease-pencil ring and a typed note; misses scar the accuracy ledger, while hints briefly move a real inspection loupe. Completing one contact sheet advances the archive route from specimen table through market, coast, mangrove ferry, and rainforest camp.

FIRST VIEWPORT: A narrow archive masthead and route rail sit above one dominant landscape contact sheet. Two images occupy most of the viewport with a slim physical gutter; the remaining count is a large stamped numeral at upper right. A compact brass tool strip sits below the pictures, while evidence notes and time remain integrated into the sheet edge rather than separate cards. The signature interaction is a shared loupe that moves over both exposures and snaps into a grease-pencil ring on a find.
